Case Study 04 / Personal · iOS app

A brutalist tip calculator, built and shipped in four weeks.

Gratuity is a free iOS tip calculator with a $2.99 Pro unlock. Designed and built solo, shipped to the App Store under Tesoro Industries LLC. It makes no network requests, collects no data, and has no account to create.

TL;DR

A solo project taken from blank file to App Store listing in four weeks — design, build, and release. Gratuity does one thing: bill in, tip and split out, in type large enough to read across a dark table. The free tier ships the calculator and one theme; a single $2.99 purchase unlocks three more themes, bill history, and a Home Screen widget. The interesting work is in the arithmetic — a round-up that rounds the total rather than the tip, and a per-person figure that rounds up so the table never comes up short.

01

One screen, no chrome

The whole app is a single screen. Bill at the top in the largest type the frame allows, tip and total beneath it, per-person below that, and a keypad filling the bottom half. Three tip presets, a nudge control for anything between them, a split stepper, and a round-up toggle. Everything else — themes, history, settings — lives behind three words in the header.

There is no onboarding, no account, no empty state to design around. Open it and the keypad is already waiting.

02

The arithmetic is the design

A tip calculator is four lines of math. What separates a good one from a bad one is what it does at the edges — where the rounding falls, and who absorbs the remainder.

Round up rounds the total, not the tip

The obvious implementation of a round-up button rounds the tip to a whole dollar. Gratuity rounds the total instead, then back-solves the tip to absorb the difference — which is what you actually want, because the total is the number you write on the slip.

On a $187.20 bill at 20%, a straight tip is $37.44 and the total lands at $224.64. With round-up engaged, the total becomes a clean $225.00 and the tip absorbs the gap at $37.80 — an effective rate of 20.19%. The label still reads 20%, because 20% is what you asked for.

The split rounds up, so the table never comes up short

Dividing a total by three rarely lands on a clean cent. Round the per-person figure down and the table collects less than the bill; the person holding the card eats the difference. Gratuity rounds up instead — the group overshoots by a cent or two and the card holder is never out of pocket.

Four settings, and no more

Default tip, default split, round up by default, and remember last used. That is the entire settings surface — no currency picker, no tax handling, no service-quality slider, no receipt scanner.

Each omission is a decision to make the common case fast rather than the rare case possible. Remember last used does most of the work a preferences screen usually does: the app converges on your habits without asking you to describe them.

03

Four themes on one skeleton

Every theme is the same layout — identical spacing, identical hit targets, identical hierarchy. What changes is the typeface, the ground, and the ink. It is the cheapest possible expression of variety, and it is the thing people pay for.

The picker previews each theme on a live total rather than a colour swatch, so the choice is made on the thing you will actually stare at.

The picker is a preview, not a palette

Each row renders the same $50.15 total in that theme's own ground, ink, and typeface. Locked themes carry a PRO tag but still show their preview in full — the thing being sold is visible before the purchase, not after it.

Concrete Free

Black pixel type on cream. The default, and the foundation the other three are cut from.

Neon Arcade Pro

Green phosphor on black. The only theme that reads as a screen rather than a page.

Diner Receipt Pro

Brown on pale green, with the solid divider swapped for a perforated one.

Banker Pro

The one break from the grid — a serif face in dark green, closer to a ledger than a terminal.

04

One purchase, no subscription

Gratuity Pro is $2.99, once. The paywall says so in type as large as the bill total, with one time, no subscription directly underneath — the objection answered before it is raised.

What the purchase unlocks is deliberately mixed: three themes (decoration), bill history (utility), and the Home Screen widget (convenience). A paywall made only of cosmetics reads as a skin store; one made only of features makes the free tier feel broken. This is neither.

The free tier is a complete tip calculator. Nothing in it is time-limited, ad-supported, or nagged at.

05

An app that never phones home

Gratuity makes no network requests of its own. Not for analytics, not for crash reporting, not for a remote config it might want later. Its only outbound traffic is Apple's StoreKit, and only when someone buys or restores Pro.

Everything the app remembers — theme, default tip, default split, bill history — lives in local storage on the device. History reaches the Home Screen widget through an App Group, Apple's shared-container mechanism, which keeps the hand-off on-device as well. Delete the app and all of it goes with it.

For a tip calculator this is not a hard engineering constraint; it is a decision about what the app is allowed to become. There is no telemetry to consult, so there is no product roadmap driven by it, and nothing to leak.

  • Network requestsNone, except Apple StoreKit for purchase and restore
  • AccountNone. There is nothing to sign in to.
  • AnalyticsNone. No SDK, no events, no identifiers.
  • StorageLocal app storage, on-device only
  • Widget transportApp Group shared container — never leaves the device
  • AdsNone, on either tier
06

Four weeks, start to listing

Built in React Native with Expo, with RevenueCat handling the single in-app purchase, and released under Tesoro Industries LLC. The scope was fixed at v1 plus the widget.

Much of what is described on this page is an absence: no tax field, no receipt scanning, no service-quality rating, no currency picker, no cloud sync. Competing tip calculators ship most of them. Gratuity shipped without.
