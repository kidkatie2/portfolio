— 02 / Momentum Score

Visualizing the model that predicts bill passage.

Two views for a probabilistic score.

TL;DR
  • Designed the UI for Passage Probability — a 0–100 score that updates as bills move.
  • Two views: a point-in-time donut breaking the score into factors, and a trajectory chart of the score over time.
  • A numbered factor list ties both views together.
Role Lead designer
Type Data visualization
Year 2025

Passage Probability: a 0–100 score that updates on every bill action, trained on 15 years of outcomes across all 50 states. I designed the two views that display it.

The problem

Teams track hundreds of bills across dozens of states. The model scores likelihood of passage from sponsor track records, political environment, bill velocity, and historical base rates.

The design

Point-in-time: a donut at the top of the bill detail panel breaks the score into factors — sponsor strength, bipartisan cosponsorship, committee makeup, similar-bill outcomes. Each factor shows its direction; factors the model can't evaluate yet are tagged "Unknown."

Fig. 02 Bill detail panel; score at top right.

Trajectory: a standalone card pairs the current score with a line chart over time, legislative actions marked. Clicking a point shows the score at that action and what changed. 30% to 70% in two weeks reads differently than flat at 55% for a month.

Fig. 03 Standalone card.

Factors are numbered so users can reference them across actions; colors match the donut.

— 03 / Keyword Alerts

Redesigning the keyword alerts system.

Three changes: query scoping, match transparency, keyword metadata.

TL;DR
  • A live Strength Indicator scores queries as users type, with projected match volume.
  • Match Type and per-result transparency show why each result matched.
  • Priority and Position fields capture what each keyword means to the user.
Role Lead designer
Status Stakeholder review
Year 2025

The problem

Customers saved overly broad keywords — insulin, education — and flooded their dashboards with irrelevant matches.

Semantic matching had just launched platform-wide: insulin now matched semiglutide results, with no indication why.

The system had no signal for which keywords mattered to a user.

Query scoping

A live Strength Indicator in the search form — modeled on a password strength meter — scores every query as it's typed. Four states, each tied to projected match volume. Strong: "~284 matches in the last 12 months · avg 24/mo, up from 15/mo the prior year." Too broad: "~47,000 matches all time · ~3,900/mo in the last 12 months."

At Fair or Too broad, the form offers narrower one-click chips — insulin pricing reform, insulin copay cap, insulin affordability act — drawn from the corpus, not a static list.

Decision: coach inside the form rather than block with a modal at save.

Fig. 04 Strong state.
Fig. 05 Too broad state, with narrower alternatives.

Match transparency

A Match Type field on every keyword: Semantic · 532 / monthly avg (topic relevance, synonyms, related concepts) vs. Exact · 29 / monthly avg (precise term in bill text). Users pick per keyword.

A dismissible banner on first visit after the semantic rollout explains the change and offers "Switch to Exact Matches only" as a one-click opt-out.

Every result card states why it matched — "Matched by: synonym / alternate term" — with the matched phrase highlighted in the snippet.

Fig. 06 Semantic vs. Exact, with monthly match volume.
Fig. 07 Match reason per card.

Keyword metadata

Two new fields on every saved keyword: Priority (how much the user cares) and Position (Support, Oppose, Watch).

Both feed 360 report ranking, recommended-bill surfacing, and daily digest ordering.

Fig. 08 Priority and Position fields.

— 06 / Reference & Search

Code Annotated, scaled from one state to fifty.

A Tennessee-only reference tool rebuilt for all 50 states.

TL;DR
  • Designed title → chapter → section navigation, with referenced bills at every level.
  • The interaction pattern holds across desktop and mobile.
Role Lead designer
Surface Desktop · Mobile web · Native mobile
Year 2024

Code Annotated started as a Tennessee-only legislative reference tool. I rebuilt it to scale across fifty distinct legal codes.

The problem

Each state codifies its laws differently — different titles, chapter numbering, and section-to-bill relationships.

The design

A branch-style interface breaks each state's code down by title, then chapter, then section, with referenced bills at every level. Users can follow a section to get alerts when new bills cite it.

Code Annotated — Title view
Fig. 12 Title view with chapter bill counts.

Scaling across surfaces

The same title/chapter/section hierarchy ships on desktop, mobile web, and native mobile. Each surface renders the depth differently over the same model.
